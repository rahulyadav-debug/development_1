The IVF Process: A Step-by-Step Guide

Ferticity IVF & Fertility Clinics · 10 min read · Reviewed by our fertility specialists

In-vitro fertilization (IVF) can feel overwhelming from the outside, but it follows a predictable, well-defined sequence. Here's what actually happens at each stage of a typical IVF cycle, from your very first consultation to the pregnancy test at the end.

1
Day 1 · Consultation

Initial Consultation & Fertility Assessment

Your journey starts with a detailed consultation with a fertility specialist, covering your medical history, previous treatments and lifestyle factors. This is usually followed by baseline tests — hormone blood work, an ultrasound to check ovarian reserve, and a semen analysis for your partner — to build a personalised treatment plan.

2
Days 2–12 · Stimulation

Ovarian Stimulation

Daily hormone injections are used to stimulate the ovaries to produce multiple mature eggs in a single cycle, instead of the one egg released naturally each month. Your specialist prescribes the specific medication and dosage based on your age, hormone levels and ovarian reserve.

3
Days 5–12 · Monitoring

Monitoring Follicle Growth

Through the stimulation phase, you'll visit the clinic every few days for transvaginal ultrasounds and blood tests. These track how many follicles are developing and how quickly, allowing your doctor to fine-tune medication doses and decide the right day for the next step.

4
~36 hours before retrieval

The Trigger Shot

Once enough follicles reach the right size, a "trigger" injection is given to complete the final maturation of the eggs. Timing is precise here — egg retrieval is scheduled almost exactly 36 hours after the trigger shot to collect the eggs at the optimal moment.

5
Day 13 · ~20 minutes

Egg Retrieval

Eggs are retrieved in a minor day-care procedure under mild IV sedation, so it isn't painful. Using ultrasound guidance, a thin needle is passed through the vaginal wall to gently aspirate fluid and eggs from each follicle. Most patients rest for an hour or two afterward and go home the same day.

6
Same day

Sperm Collection & Fertilization

A fresh or previously frozen sperm sample is prepared in the andrology lab on the same day as egg retrieval. The embryologist then fertilizes the mature eggs — either by mixing eggs and sperm together (conventional IVF) or by injecting a single sperm directly into each egg (ICSI), depending on sperm quality.

7
Days 1–5/6 after fertilization

Embryo Culture & Development

Fertilized eggs are cultured in the lab under closely controlled conditions for three to six days. Embryologists track each embryo's development daily, and by day five or six the strongest embryos typically reach the blastocyst stage, which is generally associated with the best implantation potential.

8
~5 minutes · No anaesthesia

Embryo Transfer

One (occasionally two) of the healthiest embryos is transferred into the uterus using a thin, soft catheter — a quick, painless procedure that doesn't require anaesthesia. Any remaining good-quality embryos can be frozen and stored for future cycles.

9
~14 days later

The Two-Week Wait & Pregnancy Test

The period between embryo transfer and the pregnancy test — commonly called the "two-week wait" — is often the most emotionally difficult part of the cycle. A blood test measuring beta-hCG levels, typically done around 14 days after transfer, confirms whether the cycle has resulted in a pregnancy.

This guide is for general information only and isn't a substitute for personalised medical advice. Timelines can vary based on your individual protocol and response to treatment.
